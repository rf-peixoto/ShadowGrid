How it works

There are two kinds of accounts. The game master runs the server and signs in as gamemaster. Players join through an invite link, and their account only opens the game they were invited to.

A game holds its maps, its runners, the runs (contracts) and the handouts the GM gives out, and a record of each session. Everything updates live: when the GM opens a door, resolves an attack or shares a handout, players see it at once without reloading.

The rules follow the Shadowrun Sixth World core rulebook: metatype limits and abilities, skills with specializations and expertise, every weapon, armor, implant, vehicle, spell, power, spirit and complex form from the book, dice pools with the Wild Die and Edge, actions per round and defensive interrupts, Attack Rating against Defense Rating, cover and line of sight, ammo, drain, fading and sustained spells, the Matrix with its Overwatch Score and IC, rigging, toxins and drugs, healing, and NPCs by Professional Rating. The app does the arithmetic; the table keeps the judgment calls.

It also includes the Sixth World Companion content that works with the core rules: metavariants, metasapients and the Infected as playable characters, the new qualities with their effects worked out on the sheet, contact archetypes, and every gear PACK in the store. The Companion's optional rules (alternate creation systems, changed initiative and so on) are not used.

Players

For players

The top bar has everything you need during a session. Use the tabs to switch between Map, Character, Store, Runs, Handouts and Wiki. On the right you always see your health, Edge, Karma and nuyen.

Join a game

  1. Open the invite link your GM sent you. Each link works once and expires after a week.
  2. Pick a username and a password of at least 8 characters, then click Create account & join.
  3. You land on your character sheet. Next time, sign in on the main page with the same username and password.

You can change your password any time from the menu with your name in the top right corner.

Build your runner

A new character starts as a draft. While it's a draft you edit freely, everything you buy is refunded in full if you return it, and the store sells anything up to Availability 6. When you're done, click Submit for approval. Your GM approves the sheet or sends it back with a note.

  • Metatype sets your attribute maximums and gives its abilities for free: elves and orks see in low light, dwarfs and trolls see heat, dwarfs resist toxins, orks and trolls are Built Tough, trolls have dermal deposits. The list also has every metavariant and metasapient, grouped under its base metatype (see below).
  • Magic / Resonance: Mundane, Magician, Aspected Magician, Adept, Mystic Adept or Technomancer. Magicians pick a tradition (Hermetic drains with Logic, Shamanic with Charisma) and may follow a mentor spirit.
  • Role is just a label, such as Street Samurai or Decker. It gives no bonuses in Sixth World.

Qualities that change numbers are applied for you, Companion ones included: Augmentation Acclimation and Cyborg (more Essence room), Cyber Psychosis, Cyberjack Maximization, Favored Weapon, Injury Prone, Polyglot, Twitchy, All Business and Trust Issues (Loyalty capped at 3), and the metagenic qualities: Bark, Granite and Rhino (armor), Shiva Arms (extra arms and Minor actions), Celerity and Satyr Legs (movement), Neoteny, and claws, horns, fangs, quills and tails as natural weapons. Racial-only traits can't be picked by other metatypes.

Derived values are calculated for you: Composure, Judge Intentions, Memory, Lift/Carry, movement, Initiative (plus Matrix and astral initiative), condition monitors, wound modifiers and Defense Rating.

After approval you improve with Karma: an attribute or skill costs the new rating × 5, a specialization or expertise 5, a knowledge skill 3, a spell or complex form 5, and a positive quality twice its cost. Purchases are final. Negative qualities can still be taken (a new Addiction, a SINner record…), but they give no Karma after approval, and the GM sees them in the table log. Metatype, magic type, tradition and aspect can only be changed by the GM.

Metavariants, metasapients and the Infected

The Metatype menu on the Overview lists each base metatype with its variants, then the metasapients. A variant or metasapient costs Karma at character creation, the amount shown in the menu, and you confirm before it's taken. It brings its own attribute maximums and racial qualities.

  • Metavariants (20): dwarf (Duende, Gnome, Hanuman, Koborokuru, Menehune), elf (Dalakitnon, Dryad, Nocturna, Wakyambi, Xapiri Thëpë), human (Nartaki, Valkyrie), ork (Hobgoblin, Ogre, Oni, Satyr) and troll (Cyclops, Fomorian, Giant, Minotaur). A Minotaur's horns become a natural weapon in the dice roller and in attacks, and a Nartaki's extra arms add Minor actions.
  • Metasapients: Centaur, Merrow, Naga, Pixie and Sasquatch, with their movement, size, natural weapons and armor.

Infected (HMHVV): the HMHVV card on the Overview turns a character into one of 18 Infected (ghoul, vampire, banshee, wendigo and the rest), if their base metatype allows it. The Karma cost is charged, attribute maximums change, and the character becomes Awakened and dual-natured. Technomancers can't be Infected.

  • Strain II and III set Essence to 5 and Magic maximum to 5 + initiate grade. Strain I Essence goes up and down with feeding, so the GM sets it with Set….
  • Powers and weaknesses are listed on the card. Optional powers are bought with Karma; one is free at creation when the expression allows it.
  • The Infected pay 30% more for their lifestyle. The two free physical and two free mental attribute points are added by hand (or by the GM).

Changelings use the metagenic qualities in the Qualities section (positive and negative), since SURGE only changes your qualities.

Skills and knowledge

Each skill shows its rank, a specialization (+2 dice when it applies) and an expertise (+3). Expertise grows out of a specialization once the skill is at rank 5: Make expertise turns your specialization into expertise and frees the slot for a new specialization. The pool on the right is what you roll. Some skills, like Astral or Cracking, can't be used untrained.

Below the skills, add knowledge skills (no ranks: they let you use Perception or Memory on the subject) and languages at Basic, Specialist (+2), Expert (+3) or Native. One native language is free at creation.

Contacts

Contacts are how you get gear. Each contact has a Connection and a Loyalty (1-12) and a list of what they can source: weapons, armor, cyberware, magic, vehicles, and so on. A contact can get an item whose Availability is lower than their Connection.

The contact's type already sets what they source: a fixer finds anything, an arms dealer weapons, ammo and armor, a street doc medical gear, cyberware and bioware, a talismonger magic, a decker Matrix gear, electronics and IDs, a mechanic vehicles, drones and tools, and so on. Tick extra boxes for anything else they can get you. The Companion's contact archetypes (Arms Dealer, Bartender, Deckmeister, Fence, Mr. Johnson, Street Doc, Talismonger and the rest) are in the type list too, with what each one sources. All Business and Trust Issues cap a runner's Loyalty at 3.

Store, gear and ammo

The Store tab has every item from the core book, by category: weapons, ammunition and explosives, accessories, armor, electronics, cyberdecks and software, IDs, tools, security gear, medical supplies, cyberware, bioware, magic items, vehicles and drones. Each card shows the game stats, the Availability (L means a license is needed, I means illegal) and the price. Rated items have a rating box, implants a grade (used, alphaware, betaware, deltaware), and ammo is sold in boxes of 10.

After approval, legal items up to Availability 2 are always on sale; everything else needs a contact. Untick the filter to see what a new contact would unlock: locked cards say exactly what kind of contact you'd need.

When the sellers run dry, the GM marks items out of stock: they stay in the store, greyed out, until they're back. A PACK with an out-of-stock item inside is out of stock too.

Your items appear in the Gear section of your sheet, grouped by category:

  • Equip weapons and armor. Your best body armor plus helmets, shields and implants add to your Defense Rating.
  • Reload guns from your ammo stacks, here or with the Reload button in the dice roller. Ammo fits by caliber: heavy pistol and SMG rounds, light pistol rounds, rifle rounds and so on, as named in the store. The rounds loaded and the ammo type (APDS, explosive, gel and so on) are shown and change the weapon's Attack Rating and damage.
  • Rolling an attack spends the ammo by firing mode, and throwing a grenade or throwing knife uses it up.
  • Vehicles and drones have their stats and a damage monitor. Foci are bonded from here or from the Magic section.
  • Sell gives you the game's buy-back percentage, again through a contact who could handle the item (implants included). During creation, Return refunds the full price instead.

PACKs

The PACKs category has every gear PACK from the Sixth World Companion: character PACKs (Shadowrunner Starter, Dirt Poor, Street Samurai and so on), weapon PACKs, armor, Matrix, magic, vehicle and drone PACKs, and augmentation PACKs. Open Contents to see what's inside, then Buy PACK adds every item for the PACK's price.

  • Each item in a PACK has to be reachable on its own: after approval, a PACK with a cyberdeck still needs a contact who sources cyberdecks.
  • Augmentation PACKs can be bought in the grades the book lists, at the grade's price and Essence.
  • Ammo goes with the weapon it was bought for, and items in the gear list say which PACK they came from. Their sell value is the PACK's price split over the items.
  • A few book items that aren't in the core store (drugs, some vehicle mods, the disguise kit) are added as custom items.

Magic and resonance

Awakened characters get a Magic section: Magic and its maximum, spell Attack Rating, drain pool and astral initiative; spells and rituals from the book (with drain values); adept powers with levels and a Power Point counter; foci to bond; spirits; and initiation with metamagics.

  • Casting from the dice roller rolls drain right after, and applies it: Stun, or Physical when the Drain Value is higher than your Magic. Amp Up and bigger areas add to the drain.
  • Sustained spells (duration S) are kept up automatically after you cast them, and listed under Sustained spells. Each one gives −2 dice on your other tests until you click Drop.
  • Summon rolls Conjuring + Magic against the spirit; net hits are services and the spirit's hits are your drain.
  • Initiate costs 10 + the new grade in Karma, raises your Magic maximum and adds a metamagic.
  • Implants cost Essence, and every point of Essence lost lowers Magic by 1.

Technomancers get a Resonance section instead: living persona, complex forms (threading rolls fading afterwards), sprites to compile and register, and submersion with echoes.

Matrix

The Matrix section picks your active device: a cyberdeck, a commlink or, for technomancers, the living persona. It shows Attack, Sleaze, Data Processing and Firewall, Matrix Attack and Defense Ratings, Matrix initiative for AR, cold-sim or hot-sim, the device's Matrix condition monitor and your running programs. A cyberdeck takes Data Processing and Firewall from your cyberjack.

  • Matrix actions are in the dice roller's Matrix group: Hack on the Fly, Brute Force, Data Spike, Matrix Perception and the rest, each with the right skill and attribute. Illegal actions are marked; put the target's rating in Target rating and the defender's hits raise your Overwatch Score.
  • The Overwatch Score is kept by the GM, and you don't see it. Check Overwatch Score tells you its value at that moment. At 40 the GOD converges: your device is bricked, you're dumped (dumpshock if you were in VR) and your location is reported.
  • Damage on your device costs dice on Matrix actions (−1 per 3 boxes). A technomancer's persona takes Matrix damage on the Stun track.

Vehicles and drones

Vehicles and drones you buy appear in Gear with their stats and a damage monitor. The dice roller's Vehicles group has a pool for each of them in every way you can control it:

  • At the controls: Piloting + Reaction.
  • Remote control (needs a commlink, cyberdeck or RCC): Piloting + Logic.
  • Jumped in (needs a control rig): Piloting + Intuition, plus the control rig's rating.

Drones acting on their own roll Pilot + an autosoft. Pick what each autosoft program does (Maneuvering, Targeting, Clearsight, Evasion…) in the Matrix section. The GM can put your vehicle on the map as a marker; damage it takes there shows on your sheet.

On the map

Once the GM places your token, the Map tab shows only what your runner has seen. The fog lifts as you explore, and closed doors block your sight. Icons above tokens and enemies show their statuses, such as Prone or Burning.

The map takes most of the screen. Around it:

  • Side panel (left): your condition monitors, Edge and what's around you (doors, people nearby, travel). Hide it with ‹ or [.
  • Initiative strip (top): appears when the GM shares the order. Whoever is acting glows, your own chip is outlined, and a short note tells you when it's your turn. The marks on your chip are the actions you have left this round: a diamond for the Major, dots for the Minors.
  • Dice panel (bottom): the roller on the left, the table log on the right. Collapse it with ▾ or ]; the header still shows the last roll.
  • View (top right of the map): isometric view and legend.
  • Move: drag your token, click any room you can reach (a dotted line shows the path), or use the arrow keys.
  • Doors: the buttons in the sidebar open the doors next to you, or click a door on your own cell. Locked doors need the GM.
  • Travel: standing on a stairway or portal shows a Travel button to the next map.
  • Ping: double-click a cell to flash it for the whole table.
  • Details: hover over a marker or a runner for its name, or right-click it for a card with what you can tell: what it is, how hurt it looks, its statuses, where a portal leads, and anything the GM wrote about it. Esc closes it.
  • View: Isometric view switches between the tilted view and a flat one.

Dice, combat and healing

The roller is in the dice panel under the map and in the Combat section of your sheet. Pick a pool from your sheet: a skill (with specialization or expertise), a weapon, a spell, Defense, Damage resistance and more. Your wound modifier is applied automatically. Natural weapons (claws, horns, bite) from your metatype or Infected powers are in the list too.

  • Firing modes: single shot uses 1 round; semi-auto 2 (−2 AR, +1 DV); burst 4 (−4 AR, +2 DV); full auto 10 (−6 AR).
  • Wild Die: on a 5 or 6 it counts as 3 hits; on a 1 your 5s don't count.
  • Threshold: the hits you need. The result says SUCCESS or FAILURE and shows net hits.
  • Push: spend 4 Edge before rolling to add your Edge rating, with 6s exploding. After rolling you can spend Edge on one boost: click dice to select them, then choose.
  • Defending: when you're attacked, the GM can let you Dodge, Block, Hit the Dirt or take Full Defense. It uses up your actions for the round, so keep that in mind on your turn.
  • Initiative lets you pick physical, Matrix or astral initiative, and shows your actions: 1 Major and 1 Minor, plus 1 Minor per Initiative Die (5 Minor at most). If the GM is running combat, your score goes straight into the order.

Healing follows the book, and every method is in the dice roller's Healing group: pick it, choose the patient (yourself or an ally) and roll; the healing is applied right away. First aid and medkits roll Biotech + Logic against 5 − the patient's Essence; a Heal spell rolls Sorcery + Magic. First aid without a medkit is −2 dice and can't use Edge; a medkit adds +1 (wireless) and uses one set of Medkit Supplies, which you need to have. Each works once per set of injuries and never heals drain. Rest rolls Body + Willpower per hour for Stun and Body + Body per day for Physical, with +2 when someone trained in Biotech looks after you.

Drugs and toxins: the store sells the core book's drugs and toxins by the dose (Medical). Drugs you carry can be taken from the Drugs and toxins card. You resist with Body + Willpower (untick Resist to take the full effect); each hit lowers the Power by 1. What's left applies: damage, statuses and the drug's bonuses while it lasts, then its crash. Statuses a toxin causes end when it wears off. Some substances call for an addiction test. The GM exposes you to toxins the same way.

Runs and lifestyle

The Runs tab lists the contracts the GM gave you, with their tasks and progress. Tasks marked * are mandatory. When the GM closes the run, your reward is added to your nuyen.

Your lifestyle is charged by the GM at the start of each month (Street is free, Squatter 500¥, Low 2,000¥, Middle 5,000¥, High 10,000¥, Luxury 100,000¥). If you can't pay, your nuyen goes negative: you're in debt.

The Wiki tab has rules summaries, statuses, actions, and every item, PACK, spell, power, spirit, complex form, quality, metatype, metavariant, Infected expression and NPC in the game, with a search box.

Handouts and session recaps

The Handouts tab has the notes, dossiers and pictures your GM shared with you. A badge on the tab and a short message tell you when a new one arrives.

When the GM shares a session recap, it appears at the end of the Runs tab: what happened, the fights, the awards and the GM's notes.

Print your sheet and character files

The Sheet… menu at the top of your character sheet has:

  • Print or save as PDF: a two-page printable sheet. Choose Save as PDF in the print dialog for a file.
  • Blank sheet to print: the same layout, empty, to fill in by hand.
  • Export character file: saves your runner to a file.
  • Import character file…: loads a runner from a file. It only works while your sheet is a draft, and the GM sees that the sheet was imported.
Game master

For the game master

Install

ShadowGrid runs on Linux. In its folder, run ./install.sh. It sets up Python and the dependencies, asks for the gamemaster password and asks whether to install and configure nginx for internet play (with HTTPS, a firewall rule and a service that starts on boot). Run it again any time to update.

  • ./local.sh starts a game for your home network. It prints the address for your players' computers and phones.
  • ./serve.sh starts a game for players on the internet, behind the nginx set up by the installer. If you installed the service, it's already running: use sudo systemctl restart shadowgrid instead.
  • .venv/bin/python app.py set-password changes the gamemaster password.

The README has the details: settings, the nginx hardening and how to move a game to another machine.

Start a game

  1. Start ShadowGrid with ./local.sh or ./serve.sh.
  2. Sign in with the username gamemaster and the password you chose when installing.
  3. Click New game and give it a name. Each game has its own maps, runners, runs, handouts and sessions.

Build maps

On a game, use New map to draw one from scratch or Generate map for a procedural one. The generator offers rooms and corridors, winding passages and caves, on square or hex grids, with a live preview. The same seed always produces the same map.

In Edit mode you have these tools:

  • Paint, Rectangle and Erase draw rooms.
  • Secret hides cells from the map station; players only find them by walking in.
  • Door adds doors on cell edges.
  • Marker places enemies, loot, NPCs, objectives and portals to other maps. Pick an NPC template and new markers get that NPC's stats, weapons and condition monitor. For a quick enemy, a Body and Willpower are enough for a monitor.
  • Room names a group of cells.

Delete a map from its card on the Maps tab or from Colors & settings in the editor.

Changes save automatically, and Ctrl+Z undoes them. Autosave never undoes what happened live meanwhile: doors players opened and damage or statuses from combat are kept.

Invite players

Invite player creates a single-use link that is valid for 7 days. Send one link to each player. Unused links are listed on the game page under Invite links, where you can copy or revoke them.

Approve character sheets

A submitted sheet shows a Waiting for approval tag on the home page, the game page and in the map editor. A runner with new or changed contacts shows how many are waiting for you to confirm.

  • Approve sheet locks the character into normal play, fills their Edge and confirms their contacts.
  • Send back… returns it to draft with a note the player sees.
  • As GM you can change anything on any sheet for free. Set Karma / Nuyen / Edge… sets exact values, including initiation and submersion grades.
  • Give an item… (in Gear) adds anything from the store, at any rating, grade and quantity, a whole PACK (in any of its grades), or a custom item. No nuyen and no contact needed.
  • On the Overview you can also make a runner Infected, add their optional powers and set a Strain I character's Essence. Reputation and Heat are set by you there; players only read them, and every change is logged.
  • In the Matrix section you see and change a decker's Overwatch Score, and Converge applies convergence.

The map screen

The map editor is built around the map. Everything else sits at the edges and can be tucked away:

  • Side panel (left). In Edit mode it holds the drawing tools. In Play mode it has three tabs: Map (viewing as, tools, fog, snapshots), Runners (each runner's condition, Edge, statuses and attack buttons) and Selected (the inspector of whatever you clicked; a dot marks it when something is selected). ‹ or [ hides it.
  • Combat strip (top of the map): the initiative order and the combat buttons, always in view during a fight.
  • Dice panel (bottom): your roller and the table log. ▾ or ] collapses it to one line with the last roll.
  • View (top right of the map): isometric view, legend and room names.

Run the session

Switch the map editor to Play mode during the game. It opens in the isometric view; toggle it with I.

  • See the map as: GM view shows everything. Pick a runner at the top of the Map tab, or click See as next to them on the Runners tab, to see exactly what they see (click again for the GM view). Tab cycles through them.
  • Reveal and Hide: paint a runner's fog with a brush, or reveal a whole room. The same buttons exist under Everyone for the whole party.
  • Doors: click to open or close, and Shift+click to lock.
  • Token: place or move the selected runner's token.
  • Select (the tool Play mode starts with) and Markers: drag a marker to move it, in either view (grab the icon itself in the isometric view). Click one to open its inspector in the Selected tab: its NPC stat block, its condition monitor, its statuses, attack buttons, and a description players read when they right-click it.
  • Statuses: under each runner in the Runners tab and in a marker's inspector, add Prone, Burning, Zapped, Cover and the rest. Their icons appear on the map for everyone. Only you can set and clear them.
  • Snapshots: save and restore the whole game state (positions, exploration and doors).

NPC templates cover every grunt in the core book from Professional Rating 0 to 10, the critters, spirits of any Force, IC by type and host rating, every vehicle and drone in the book, the runners' own vehicles and drones (their damage stays in sync with the sheet), the Companion's contact archetypes (for when a contact has to fight or be rolled for) and the Infected. Apply NPC template… in the inspector turns any marker into one.

Combat

The combat strip above the map runs the initiative tracker. Start combat… picks the runners and enemies on this map and rolls the NPCs' initiative. Players' own initiative rolls land in the order automatically.

  • Each chip shows the actions left this round: a diamond for the Major Action and dots for the Minor ones (1 + Initiative Dice, 5 at most). Attacks and defenses spend them for you; a new round gives them back. Initiative isn't rerolled between rounds.
  • Click a chip for its menu: set the initiative score, spend a Major or Minor Action by hand (Use Major, Use Minor, Reset), make it their turn, show them on the map, roll initiative, attack with them or attack them, hide or reveal an enemy, or remove them from combat.
  • Players see it shares the order on their map. Hidden enemies stay off their strip until you reveal them.
  • 🔥 Ongoing damage appears when someone is Burning, Corrosive or Poisoned: at the start of a round, everyone affected resists the damage at once.
  • Next turn › moves on; Roll NPCs, + Add (including custom combatants), New round and End (with a confirmation) are next to it.

Attacks are resolved by you, with every roll made by the server. Open ⚔ Attack… from the strip (it starts with whoever is acting), a chip, a runner or a marker.

  • Targets: add more with + Add a target. Several targets split the dice pool evenly and cost a Minor Action; Anticipation (4 Edge) rolls the full pool against each.
  • Line of sight and cover are worked out from the map for each target: walls and closed doors between attacker and target give Cover I to IV, and with no line of sight at all the attack is refused. Change the cover per target, or tick Ignore line of sight for indirect fire.
  • Defense: per target, pick an interrupt the defender takes: Block (+Close Combat, melee only), Dodge (+Athletics), Hit the Dirt (+2 dice against ranged attacks, then Prone) or Full Defense (+Willpower for the rest of the round). They cost the defender's actions; the menu says when they can't afford one.
  • Called shots: +2 DV for −4 dice, or the Edge actions Shank (melee), Vitals and Disarm. Intercept is a melee attack out of turn on someone moving past.
  • Counterspelling: a magician protecting the target adds their Sorcery to the Defense test against a spell.
  • Astral and Matrix attacks use the right pools: Astral + Willpower in astral combat, and Cracking with the deck's Attack in the Matrix, including IC. Matrix damage hits the device, and Black IC, Blaster and Sparky add biofeedback.
  • Edge: Award Edge gives 1 Edge to whoever's Attack or Defense Rating is 4 or more ahead, at most 2 per round. Edge costs (called shots, Anticipation) are taken from the attacker.
  1. The attacker rolls their weapon pool (runners use their sheet, NPCs their template), with statuses such as Blinded or Fatigued applied. The runner's ammo is spent.
  2. The target defends with Reaction + Intuition (Willpower + Intuition against direct spells, Reaction + Willpower against indirect ones), plus cover and the chosen interrupt. Prone is +2 against distant attacks and −2 up close. Ties go to the attacker.
  3. The Damage Value is the weapon's DV + net hits (+ firing mode, ammo and called shot); the target soaks with Body. Direct spells can't be soaked.
  4. The rest goes to the right monitor, Stun overflowing into Physical. A combat spell also rolls the caster's drain.

Under Modifiers you can add dice to any test, extra DV or Amp Up, override an NPC's pool, use the Wild Die, roll unopposed against weak grunts, keep the roll hidden, or roll without applying damage. Ignore action limits lets someone attack without actions left. The full breakdown goes to the table log.

Give out runs

On the Runs tab, New run asks for a name, a reward per runner, a description the players see, the tasks and the runners who take the job.

Tick tasks as they happen. The payout scales with the share of tasks done, and every mandatory task must be ticked before the run can be completed. Complete & pay… suggests the payout (you can change it) and pays it to each assigned runner.

Store stock

The Store tab lists everything the store sells, PACKs included. Untick an item when the sellers run out: players see it out of stock and can't buy it. Tick it again when it's back. Search, filter by category or by stock, and mark everything shown in or out of stock at once. You can still give out-of-stock items from a sheet.

Handouts

The Handouts tab holds notes, dossiers, maps and pictures for the players. New handout asks for a title, the text in Markdown (headings, bold, lists, quotes and links, with a live preview), an image and who gets it: everyone or chosen runners. Untick Show it to them now to prepare a handout before the session and reveal it later.

Sessions

On the Sessions tab, Start session when the table sits down and end it afterwards. Everything in between (fights, attacks, notable rolls, Karma and nuyen, runs and handouts) becomes a recap with a timeline. Add your own notes in Markdown, tick Share with players to show it on their Runs tab, or Export (.md) it.

Toxins and drugs

Toxins & drugs… in the Between runs bar opens the game's library. Built in are the core rulebook's toxins (CS/Tear Gas, Gamma-Scopolamine, Narcoject, Nausea Gas, Neuro-Stun VIII to X, Pepper Punch, Seven-7) and drugs (Alcohol, Bliss, Cram, Deepweed, Jazz, Kamikaze, Long Haul, Nitro, Novacoke, Psyche, Zen), plus the Companion's Renfield and HMHVV strains. Their effects are applied, not just described:

  • damage equal to the Power left after resisting, and statuses (Dazed, Nauseated, Blinded I, Immobilized…) that end when the effect wears off;
  • attribute changes, Initiative Dice, Perception, High Pain Tolerance, dice on all tests or on Physical tests, Psyche's lighter penalty for sustained spells;
  • crashes: unresisted damage, statuses, and penalties that last on their own (Nitro's −2 dice, Novacoke's Charisma and Willpower at 0).

Add your own substances with the same fields, or Copy a built-in one to change it for your table. Expose a runner from the Drugs and toxins card in the Combat section of their sheet; the effect stays listed there until you click Wears off.

Money, Karma and Edge

  • These are in the Between runs bar of the Runners tab.
  • Award Karma / Nuyen: give to one runner, several or everyone. Negative amounts take it away. Each award has a reason the runner sees in their history.
  • Charge monthly lifestyle: each runner pays their lifestyle (SINner and a suprathyroid gland cost more). Nuyen can go negative.
  • Store buys at …%: what players get when they sell gear.
  • Edge: only you refresh or award Edge. Use −, + and ↻ next to each runner, or Refresh everyone's Edge. In combat, Award Edge in the attack dialog gives the Edge earned from Attack Rating against Defense Rating.
  • History: every Karma and nuyen change in the game.

Backups

ShadowGrid backs everything up once a day, keeping the last 10. Backups on the home page lists them: Back up now, download one, restore one, or restore from a file you downloaded. Restoring puts every game, map, runner and image back to that moment and signs everyone out. The current state is saved first, so a restore can be undone.

Rules reference

Dice

  • A 5 or 6 is a hit. Glitch: more than half of the dice show 1. Critical glitch: a glitch with no hits.
  • Wild Die: on a 5 or 6 it counts as 3 hits; on a 1, none of the 5s in the roll count.
  • An untrained skill rolls the attribute −1. Specialization +2, expertise +3.

You can hold at most 7 Edge. The GM refreshes and awards it; in combat you can also gain up to 2 per round from Attack Rating against Defense Rating.

Combat

  • Ranges: Close 0-3 m, Near 4-50 m, Medium 51-250 m, Far 251-500 m, Extreme 500+ m. No Attack Rating at a range means the weapon can't reach.
  • Defense Rating = Body + armor (best body armor, plus helmet, shield, implants and qualities).
  • Damage after soaking higher than Body knocks the target Prone. Electricity adds Zapped, fire Burning, cold Chilled, chemicals Corrosive.
  • Actions per round: 1 Major, 1 Minor + 1 per Initiative Die (max 5). 4 Minor make a Major.
  • Cover I to IV adds its level to the Defense Rating and the Defense test.
  • Each spell you sustain: −2 dice on other tests.

Matrix

  • Legal actions use Electronics, illegal ones Cracking. Attack Rating = Attack + Sleaze; Defense Rating = Data Processing + Firewall.
  • Overwatch Score: +1 per hit the defender rolls against an illegal action. Convergence at 40.
  • Matrix damage: −1 die on Matrix actions per 3 boxes on the device.

Toxins and drugs

  • Resist with Body + Willpower: each hit lowers the Power by 1, and what's left applies.
  • Damage equals the Power left. Statuses last until the effect wears off.
  • A drug's crash applies when it wears off: unresisted damage, statuses, or penalties for a while.
  • After approval, a negative quality gives no Karma.

Condition monitors

  • Physical: 8 + Body ÷ 2. Stun: 8 + Willpower ÷ 2, rounded up. −1 die per 3 boxes on each track.
  • A full Stun track knocks you out, and further Stun overflows into Physical.
  • A full Physical track means dying. Overflow boxes equal Body × 2; filling them is death.

Availability

  • Character creation: up to Availability 6.
  • After approval: legal items up to 2 are always available; otherwise you need a confirmed contact who covers the goods with a Connection higher than the Availability.
  • Implant grades: used (Essence ×1.1, price ×0.5, Availability −1), alphaware (×0.8, ×1.2, +1), betaware (×0.7, ×1.5, +2), deltaware (×0.5, ×2.5, +3).

Sixth World Companion

  • Metavariants and metasapients cost Karma at creation (shown in the metatype menu). Changing back refunds it.
  • Infected cost Karma by expression; non-human bases may pay more and Awakened characters less, as the book says. Lifestyle costs ×1.3.
  • A PACK costs its listed price (or its grade price), and every item still has to be available to you.
  • Optional rules from the Companion are not used: creation stays on the core-book priority system, and initiative, Edge and contacts work as in the core book.
